### Abstract
Marine dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC<sub>total</sub>) is a key component of the global ocean carbon cycle. Over recent decades, DIC<sub>total</sub> has increased due to rising anthropogenic CO<sub>2</sub>, but the response from the biological carbon pump (BCP), which transfers carbon from the surface to the ocean interior, remains uncertain. Here, we reconstruct global three-dimensional DIC changes from 2004 to 2024 down to 2km and separate anthropogenic, biological, and carbonate contributions. We find that while DIC<sub>total</sub> increased throughout the water column, driven primarily by anthropogenic CO<sub>2</sub>, the global inventory of biologically respired carbon associated with the BCP remained statistically constant. This apparent stability masks pronounced regional and vertical redistribution of respired carbon within the ocean interior. In some regions, respired carbon accumulated at shallower depths, reducing its isolation from the atmosphere, whereas in others it was transported deeper, enhancing long-term storage. These contrasting patterns co-vary with changes in ocean stratification and ventilation, indicating that physical change may regulate where biologically respired carbon is retained. Although these redistributions had little net effect on the global carbon inventory over the study period, they reveal a structural reorganization of the BCP that may influence the ocean’s capacity to sequester carbon under continued climate change.

 
### Analysis and plotting
This repository contains the raw data and scripts used to generate the results presented in the manuscript. The Jupyter Notebook Figures and numbers includes the main visualizations and statistical outputs. For deeper exploration, interactive versions are available via Plotly.
