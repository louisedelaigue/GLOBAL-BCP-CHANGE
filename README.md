### Abstract
Marine dissolved inorganic carbon (DICtotal) is a key component of the global ocean carbon cycle. Over recent decades, DICtotal has increased due to rising anthropogenic CO2, but the response from the biological carbon pump (BCP), which transfers carbon from the surface to the ocean interior, remains uncertain. Here, we reconstruct global three-dimensional DIC changes from 2004 to 2024 down to 2 km depth and distinguish contributions from anthropogenic, organic matter remineralization, and calcium carbonate cycling. We find that while DICtotal increased throughout the water column, driven primarily by anthropogenic CO2, the global inventory of biologically respired carbon associated with the BCP remained constant. However, this apparent stability masked pronounced regional and vertical redistribution of respired carbon within the ocean interior. In some regions, respired carbon accumulated at shallower depths, reducing its isolation from the atmosphere, whereas in others it was transported deeper, enhancing long-term storage. These contrasting patterns co-vary with changes in upper-ocean stratification and with the ventilation state of the ocean interior, indicating that both regulate where biologically respired carbon is retained. Although these redistributions did not impact the net global carbon inventory over the study period, they reveal a structural reorganization of the BCP that may influence the ocean’s capacity to sequester carbon under continued climate change.

 
### Analysis and plotting
This repository contains the raw data and scripts used to generate the results presented in the manuscript. The Jupyter Notebook Figures and numbers includes the main visualizations and statistical outputs. For deeper exploration, interactive versions are available via Plotly.
